When an association describes what it offers in generic terms ("we support," "we empower," "we develop"), the other party struggles to know what they'll actually receive. A product, by contrast, defines itself: who it's for, the problem, the output, the timeframe, and the next step.

## What changes when you own a product?

- **For the funder**: a defined output they can evaluate and fund.
- **For the beneficiary**: clarity on what they'll get and when.
- **For the association**: the service repeats and improves instead of being reinvented each time.

## From service to product

At Jadeel we start by fixing a unified structure for each product, then turn the association's accumulated experience into **knowledge products and paid services** that can be sold and sponsored. That's what the **Social Products Lab** does.

A product doesn't erase the association's mission — it makes it repeatable, measurable and sustainable.
