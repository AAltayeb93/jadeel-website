Many annual reports open with "we ran X events and Y people benefited." Those are **activity** numbers, not **impact** numbers. Activity is what we do; output is what it directly produces; impact is the change in a beneficiary's life because of the intervention.

## The chain from resources to impact

- **Inputs**: the resources we put in (budget, volunteers, time).
- **Activities**: what we run (workshops, distribution, programs).
- **Outputs**: the directly countable result (number of beneficiaries, kits delivered).
- **Impact**: the actual change (a skill improved, a need reduced, economic empowerment).

## Why does this distinction matter?

Because funders and partners don't fund the activity itself — they fund the change it creates. When a report stays at the activity level, it's hard for a supporter to decide whether to scale or continue.
